# Quantified Headline Findings — Transit Supply Intelligence

> **Audited Timestamp:** `2026-10-10T17:36:37.732785`  
> **Scope:** 50 routes | 147 active stops | 500 scheduled trips

---

### 1. 68.2% of daily trips are compressed into 6 rush hours, creating a 6.5× departure drop off-peak
- **Primary Metric:** `68.2%` (6.5× gap)
- **Empirical Evidence:** 341 of 500 trips depart between 07:00-10:00 (168 trips) and 17:00-20:00 (173 trips). Peak departure rate is 56.8 trips/hr across the network, dropping to 8.8 trips/hr during off-peak windows.
- **Strategic / Business Implication:** Off-peak service intervals leave midday and late-shift riders with long waiting intervals, discouraging non-commute transit use.

### 2. Top 10 transfer hubs carry 11.2% of all network route connections across just 6.8% of stops
- **Primary Metric:** `11.2%` (Top 10 of 147 hubs)
- **Empirical Evidence:** The top 10 interchange hubs handle 112 of 1000 total route-stop intersection pairs. Nungambakkam leads the network with 13 intersecting lines, followed by Washermanpet and ECR (12 lines each).
- **Strategic / Business Implication:** High spatial concentration creates critical single points of failure: terminal congestion or delay propagation at these 10 hubs cascades network-wide.

### 3. 16.0% of scheduled routes (8 corridors) have zero Saturday and Sunday service
- **Primary Metric:** `16.0%` (8 corridors)
- **Empirical Evidence:** 8 of 50 routes operate strictly under weekday service calendars (c_weekday), including express commuter lines 500A, E4, E3, E5, M79.
- **Strategic / Business Implication:** Weekend workforce, retail commuters, and suburban travelers face complete supply shutoffs on these corridors, suppressing non-workday farebox potential.

### 4. 82.0% of routes face off-peak headways of 4.5+ hours (>=270 min) due to schedule concentration
- **Primary Metric:** `82.0%` (438.1 min avg off-peak)
- **Empirical Evidence:** While base daily trip count is uniformly 10 trips/route, peak-heavy allocation leaves 41 routes with off-peak headways exceeding 270 minutes, stretching up to 1080.0 minutes on routes allocating 9-10 trips to peak hours.
- **Strategic / Business Implication:** Corridors with severe off-peak intervals represent prime candidates for supply-neutral schedule smoothing.

### 5. Route transfer exposure varies 2.6× (7 to 18 connected lines), yet all corridors receive identical trip volumes
- **Primary Metric:** `2.6× span` (7 to 18 lines)
- **Empirical Evidence:** Highly integrated corridors (Route 2A, 58, E5) connect with 18 distinct routes across their stops, while isolated lines connect with only 7 routes. Despite this 2.6× connectivity differential, the timetable allocates exactly 10 trips to every route.
- **Strategic / Business Implication:** Uniform supply allocation indicates rigid schedule design rather than demand- or connectivity-weighted resource deployment.

### 6. Peak holiday travel (Diwali at 3.0× multiplier) projects a supply surge requirement of +1000 extra bus runs
- **Primary Metric:** `+1000` (3.0× surge)
- **Empirical Evidence:** Under baseline planning assumptions across 15 regional celebrations, festival surges project system-wide fleet requirements expanding by +40 to +1000 supplemental runs.
- **Strategic / Business Implication:** Capacity gaps of this magnitude exceed normal operational maintenance reserves and require pre-emptive depot staging and holiday rosters.
